# RODIC InfraAI Innovation Challenge 2026

## AI-Assisted Infrastructure Visual Inspection

An evidence-grounded multimodal AI proof of concept for infrastructure visual inspection.

The system combines **domain-trained computer vision**, **multimodal visual reasoning**, and **explicit evidence arbitration** to transform infrastructure images into structured, traceable inspection intelligence while keeping consequential engineering decisions under qualified human validation.

> **Core principle:** AI observes, organizes, and reconciles visual evidence; qualified humans validate consequential engineering decisions.

---

## 1. Business Problem

Infrastructure inspection is often:

- Manual and time-consuming
- Dependent on individual inspector experience
- Difficult to scale across large image collections
- Difficult to standardize
- Vulnerable to inconsistent visual interpretation
- Disconnected from machine-readable evidence and audit trails

The objective is not simply to detect a crack.

The objective is to create an **evidence-grounded inspection intelligence layer** that can organize multiple visual signals and clearly communicate uncertainty.

## 4. CODEBRIM Evaluation Results

CODEBRIM is evaluated as a multi-label classification problem. Therefore, subset accuracy and F1 metrics should be interpreted differently from ordinary single-label classification accuracy.

### Overall Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Macro F1 | **82.56%** |
| Micro F1 | **82.80%** |
| Subset Accuracy | **69.16%** |

**Important:** Subset accuracy requires the complete set of predicted labels for an image to match the ground truth. It should not be described as ordinary single-label classification accuracy.

### Per-Class Results

| Class | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Background | 87.50% | 98.00% | 92.45% |
| Crack | 93.75% | 70.47% | 80.46% |
| Spallation | 84.80% | 75.71% | 80.00% |
| Efflorescence | 78.40% | 69.50% | 73.68% |
| Exposed bars | 98.28% | 80.28% | 88.37% |
| Corrosion staining | 76.88% | 84.25% | 80.39% |

The strongest class by F1 is **exposed bars**, while **efflorescence** is currently the most challenging class.

### Interpretation

The results demonstrate that the domain-trained model can identify multiple infrastructure defect categories with useful precision and recall. The difference between subset accuracy and macro/micro F1 also highlights the difficulty of simultaneously identifying every applicable defect label in a multi-label image.

The evaluation is intended to demonstrate technical feasibility of the inspection-assistance approach rather than replace qualified engineering inspection.

## 6. Innovation: Evidence Arbitration

The key innovation of the PoC is not simply combining multiple AI models. It is the explicit separation, preservation, and arbitration of evidence produced by different sources.

The domain-trained computer vision models provide task-specific defect evidence, while the Gemini multimodal reasoning layer provides broader visual interpretation. These sources are kept distinct before an inspection conclusion is generated.

### Evidence Arbitration Flow

```text
Domain CV Evidence
        |
        +------------------+
                           |
                           v
                    Evidence Layer
                           ^
                           |
Gemini Visual Evidence ---+
                           |
                           v
                  Evidence Arbitration
                           |
             +-------------+-------------+
             |             |             |
             v             v             v
          Agreement      Conflict    Insufficient
             |             |             |
             +-------------+-------------+
                           |
                           v
              Inspection Intelligence
                           |
                           v
                    Human Validation
```

### Evidence States

| State | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| `HIGH_AGREEMENT` | Independent evidence sources substantially agree |
| `PARTIAL_AGREEMENT` | Evidence is useful but incomplete |
| `MODEL_CONFLICT` | Evidence sources disagree and require review |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | Available visual evidence is inadequate for a reliable conclusion |

### Why This Matters

Infrastructure inspection involves consequential decisions where an unsupported AI conclusion can create operational and engineering risk. The system therefore does not treat a single model output as ground truth.

Instead, the architecture preserves:

- Which model produced the evidence
- What defect or visual characteristic was detected
- The associated model confidence
- Whether independent evidence sources agree
- Whether evidence is conflicting or insufficient
- Whether human validation is required

This creates an evidence-grounded inspection workflow rather than a black-box classification workflow.

### Confidence Handling

The PoC deliberately avoids blindly averaging confidence values from heterogeneous models and presenting the result as a calibrated probability.

Source-specific confidence remains attached to its originating model. The arbitration layer uses the relationship between evidence sources to determine whether evidence is supportive, conflicting, or insufficient.

This design improves traceability and provides a clearer basis for human review.

## 7. Responsible AI

The PoC is designed as an **AI-assisted inspection system**, not as an autonomous engineering decision-maker.

### Human-in-the-Loop Principle

```text
AI observes
    |
    v
AI organizes evidence
    |
    v
AI identifies agreement / conflict / uncertainty
    |
    v
Qualified human reviews consequential findings
    |
    v
Engineering decision
```

The system supports inspectors and engineers by organizing visual evidence and highlighting potential defects. Final consequential decisions remain with appropriately qualified personnel.

### Responsible AI Controls

| Control | Implementation in the PoC |
|---|---|
| Human oversight | Consequential findings require qualified human validation |
| Evidence traceability | Model-specific evidence is preserved before arbitration |
| Uncertainty handling | Conflicting and insufficient evidence are explicitly represented |
| Provenance | Dataset, evaluation split, metrics, and model artifacts are documented |
| Reproducibility | Evaluation configuration and evidence files are retained |
| Transparency | Model limitations and evaluation methodology are documented |
| Data governance | Raw datasets and credentials are excluded from the public repository |
| Security | API keys and model checkpoints are not committed to GitHub |

### Uncertainty and Escalation

The system is designed to avoid forced conclusions when available evidence is inadequate.

When evidence sources disagree or visual evidence is insufficient, the system can route the case for human review rather than treating uncertainty as a confident prediction.

### Decision Boundary

The PoC does **not** claim to determine structural safety, structural integrity, repair requirements, load-bearing capacity, or regulatory compliance autonomously.

Its intended role is to provide **inspection assistance, evidence organization, defect indication, and prioritization support**.

## 9. Infrastructure Domain Taxonomy

The PoC is designed around visual indicators that commonly occur in infrastructure inspection imagery. The taxonomy separates observable visual defects from higher-level engineering interpretation.

### Current Visual Defect Taxonomy

| Category | Visual Indicator | Current Model |
|---|---|---|
| Crack | Visible cracking or fracture patterns | CODEBRIM + SDNET2018 |
| Spallation | Localized concrete surface loss or deterioration | CODEBRIM |
| Efflorescence | Visible crystalline or salt-like surface deposits | CODEBRIM |
| Exposed bars | Visible reinforcement bars exposed through concrete loss | CODEBRIM |
| Corrosion staining | Visible staining associated with possible reinforcement corrosion | CODEBRIM |

### Observation vs Engineering Interpretation

The system distinguishes between what is visually observable and what requires engineering interpretation.

```text
Visual Observation
       |
       v
AI Defect Indication
       |
       v
Evidence + Uncertainty
       |
       v
Human Engineering Interpretation
       |
       v
Engineering Decision
```

For example, a model may identify a crack-like visual pattern. Determining the structural significance, cause, propagation risk, or required remedial action is outside the autonomous decision boundary of the current PoC.

### Taxonomy Extension

The taxonomy is intentionally extensible. Future versions can add additional infrastructure-specific categories, metadata, localization information, severity criteria, and contextual evidence without changing the fundamental evidence-arbitration architecture.

## 19. Limitations

The current implementation is a proof-of-concept and has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results.

### Dataset Limitations

- Public benchmark datasets may not fully represent real-world infrastructure inspection conditions.
- Field imagery can differ substantially in lighting, viewpoint, surface condition, camera quality, and defect appearance.
- The current POC has not established performance on a large organization-specific field dataset.

### Model Limitations

- The current computer vision models are classification-oriented rather than full structural assessment systems.
- The POC does not independently determine structural severity or structural safety.
- Model performance can degrade under domain shift.
- Small, occluded, ambiguous, or visually similar defects may be difficult to classify reliably.

### Evaluation Limitations

- CODEBRIM evaluation is based on the available validation split.
- SDNET2018 uses a reconstructed deterministic evaluation split because the original validation indices were not preserved in the training checkpoint.
- Reported benchmark results should not be interpreted as production field performance.
- Runtime measurements were obtained in the POC environment and should not be treated as production latency benchmarks.

### Multimodal Reasoning Limitations

Multimodal reasoning models can produce plausible but incorrect interpretations. Their outputs therefore remain evidence sources rather than authoritative engineering conclusions.

The evidence-arbitration layer is intended to make disagreement and insufficient evidence visible rather than conceal uncertainty behind a single generated answer.

### Operational Limitations

The current POC does not yet provide:

- Production-grade monitoring
- Enterprise authentication and authorization
- Long-term inspection history management
- Automated model-drift detection
- Formal engineering certification
- Fully validated field deployment

These limitations define the boundary between the submitted POC and a future production system.

A conventional computer vision pipeline may answer:

> What defect does the model see?

The proposed architecture extends this toward:

> What evidence exists, where does it come from, do independent sources agree, how uncertain is the evidence, and should a qualified human review it?

This shift from isolated prediction toward evidence-grounded inspection intelligence is the central architectural proposition of the POC.

### Practical Innovation

The solution is intentionally designed to be extensible. Additional models, sensors, inspection modalities, or domain-specific reasoning components can be incorporated as additional evidence sources without requiring the entire architecture to become a single monolithic model.

This provides a path from a focused visual-inspection POC toward a broader infrastructure intelligence platform while preserving the human decision boundary.
